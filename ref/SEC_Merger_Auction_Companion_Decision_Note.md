## 1. Formality and conditions: preserve the later clarification

The older instruction says any range is informal and uses final-round participation or agreement markups as indicators of formality (I §3.5, p.7). Alex later expressly retains Mac-Gray Party A's **$18–19 best-and-final range as formal** and says exclusivity should not downgrade a formal offer (V II.6–7; W `Z6953`, `V6953:W6953`; MG BG-054, p.36). That is a later substantive clarification, not merely an unresolved spreadsheet inconsistency.

**Recommendation:** assess formality using documentation and solicitation context; retain source terms such as IOI, LOI and non-binding separately. Assess completion conditionality independently. This implements V I.10, II.5–7 and VIII.6 rather than forcing one “formal-with-conditions” label to encode several concepts.

The **none/light/heavy thresholds are my recommendation**, not a rubric already fixed by Alex. “None” requires affirmative support; a markup or silence is insufficient. “Less conditional” is a relative comparison, not proof of low absolute conditionality. Keep payoff uncertainty separate: MG Party B's additional $2.50 was its valuation of **equity options**, not guaranteed cash and not simply a cash earnout (MG BG-054–056, pp.36–37; W `AG6954`). This distinction preserves the intended comparison between price and deal certainty without expanding into the separate comprehensive legal-terms project.

## 2. Counts, anonymous bidders and price bounds: one explicit convention needs replacement

I §3.5 asks collectors to copy a group-wide X–Y envelope onto each unidentified bidder. W `AG6390` asks whether Zep's aggregate $20–22 group should become five bidder rows, including three with `[20,22]`. That comment is a question, but the earlier general expansion rule is explicit.

**Proposed change requiring approval:** retain the group envelope, known members and disjoint residual cohorts in the primary extraction. Where an individual amount can only be bounded by the group envelope, label that as an **analyst-inferred admissible interval**, not the range actually submitted by that bidder. A legacy expanded view can be generated, but must carry that distinction and must not manufacture cross-round identities. The information is retained; its evidentiary meaning changes from misleading individual ranges to explicitly bounded observations.

Mac-Gray illustrates why this matters: twenty NDA signers comprise **two strategic bidders, A and CSC/Pamplona, and eighteen financial bidders including B and C**. Thus the residual after identifying those four is sixteen financial bidders, not twenty additional signers or two additional anonymous strategics (MG BG-024, BG-026–027, BG-030, BG-037, pp.32–34; V II.2, II.4).

Keep disclosed totals and derived counts distinct. A residual calculated from twenty and then added back is an arithmetic partition, not independent verification of twenty. Preserve literal count qualifiers: V's concern about inappropriate “at least” labels does not justify changing a genuinely qualified source statement into an exact count. W `AG6069` itself leaves “several” unresolved.

## 4. Rounds and chronology: decisions, implementation and approximation

Alex wants rounds inferred from substantive process transitions, not copied from bankers' informal/formal labels; he also wants deadline extensions and non-enforcement visible (V V.3, VIII.5, “Alex's summary” A–B). Conversely, he explicitly treats PetSmart's December improvement request as continuation of round 2 (V III.6). Consequently, **“every rebid request creates a round” would be wrong**.

**Recommendation:** use changes in admission, information stage and negotiation objective, with a stated rationale. Preserve authorization and implementation separately. Mac-Gray's June 24 decision can anchor the launch assessment, but actual fifty-party outreach occurred during the following weeks, not wholly on June 24 (MG BG-020–024, pp.30–32; compare V II.2). July 25 and September 11 are strong subsequent transition candidates (MG BG-034–035, BG-051); the September exclusivity and October extension do not mechanically require additional auction rounds.

The original instructions allow rough dates and emphasize sequence, including a mid-February representative date of February 15 (I p.2). They do not establish a complete universal interpolation rule. The guide therefore labels midpoint assignment and early/mid/late calendar bands as **recommended conventions**, preserves literal dates and ranges, and requires supported precedence to govern ordering. A plausible assigned date must never masquerade as a reported date or overwrite a late submission. A source typo such as MG BG-037's “2103” remains in the quotation with a separate correction, not an invented future event.

Keep Alex's mandatory round/deadline, count, type, exit, affiliation and scope checks; retain pilot conditionality review. However, adjudicating disputed facts in the other six reference deals would require their actual filings; their workbook annotations alone are not sufficient.
