## Process and round analysis

### One process

The Sanofi proposal starts the recorded attempt. Its rejection does not end all participation or establish that the sale effort ended.

Pfizer's contacts continue in May. The June admission, July NDA disclosure, and August auction follow within one reported sequence.

E5 requires all three restart conditions. The source does not satisfy them. No Process terminated or Process restarted row is supported.

The withdrawal of Sanofi's consent solicitation concerns board replacement. It is not withdrawal of Sanofi's acquisition participation.

### Round 1: the start date is not fully reported

The source reports several NDAs by 5 July. It does not date a general outreach decision or describe the first contact with every party.

The two most defensible anchors are:

- **28 June:** the last dated target sale step before Pfizer's NDA. E6's undated-outreach proxy can apply if the several-party process implies unreported outreach.
- **29 June:** Pfizer's first NDA and explicit sale-process invitation. This fits the bilateral fallback if no wider target outreach is established.

A 5 July start mistakes public disclosure for NDA execution or the actual start. A later 19 July start ignores the prior admission and NDAs.

The 28 June interpretation requires an inferred opening and the mandatory **Process:** Question under Part F. The 29 June reported invitation needs a boundary Review item.

The first priced proposal remains in round 0 under either interpretation. The round ends with the 10 August admission decision.

Round 1's known in-process bidders include Sanofi, Pfizer, and unnamed NDA parties. Exact counts and types of the unnamed parties are absent.

The source reports one bidder's round-1 proposal: Pfizer. Sanofi's earlier $52.50 proposal does not become a second round-1 bid without a reaffirmation.

The 8 August due date is operative and reached. The next-stage decision makes its outcome **Enforced** under E9.

### Round 2: 10 August is a firm boundary

The target expressly admits Pfizer and several others to a subsequent round. Expanded access and the price-and-documents request follow.

This meets E6's documented stage-admission rule. The 11 August access and 14 August letter do not each open another round.

The source establishes Pfizer's membership. It does not identify the other admitted parties or give an exact number.

The first offer request uses “definitive proposal”, not “non-binding preliminary proposal”. Its meaning determines finality and the later round structure.

### Two coherent final-stage readings

**Preferred strict reading:** “definitive” alone does not expressly announce finality under E6. The 10 August stage therefore starts as **Not final**.

Pfizer submits its $77 proposal on 19 August. The later request that day expressly seeks a best-and-final proposal.

Under E6(b), that request opens **round 3**, **Announced as final**. Its due date is 20 August. The $81.50 bid belongs there.

Under this reading, the 19 August deadline outcome is **Enforced**, because the target opens the final stage after receipt of the proposal.

**Defensible alternative:** “definitive proposal” requests an executable final offer. The first request implements the admitted stage and gives round 2 finality.

Under that reading, the 19 August request continues round 2. E6 prohibits another boundary inside a round already announced as final.

The two Pfizer prices then belong to round 2. The later due date may be an extension, subject to E9's order of target actions.

A reviewer must inspect the workbook's explanation and deadline treatment together. The source wording alone does not justify multiple penalties for one coherent finality choice.

Neither reading makes the $77 proposal Informal. The price-and-documents response independently satisfies route 1.

### No separate round from other events

- The 19 July letter implements the first process stage; its conditional future shortlist does not itself admit anyone.
- The 18 August markup is Pfizer's response, not a target stage decision.
- Pfizer's internal board approval does not select Pfizer for definitive negotiation on Medivation's behalf.
- The 19–20 August contract negotiations do not prove an earlier target decision than the reported final-stage request.
- The final board approval and agreement end the contest. They do not open another final round.
- The source reports no exclusivity period, renewed approach after exclusivity, go-shop, or post-agreement rival proposal.

The earlier cohort comprises NDA signers. The later cohort comprises admitted parties. The source does not equate their memberships or totals.

The phrase “subsequent round” supports continuity of the auction. It does not establish which anonymous participants survived or how many did not advance.

### The $65 proposal's diligence forecast

The source says Pfizer expected completion of diligence and a definitive agreement in approximately one week. See p. 23, L1493–1494.

This forecast is evidence under amended Part B. It excludes an H2 trigger based on a reported two-week period.

It does not say “confirmatory”, “limited”, or “expedited”. E12 does not state that every period shorter than two weeks produces **Light**.

**Unclear** is the conservative literal application. **Light** can reflect an interpretation of the one-week forecast as expedited diligence.

This distinction needs review against the wording, not model consensus. **Heavy** based only on the diligence condition or non-binding label is unsupported.

The later expansion of diligence does not make the 8 August bid Formal. Nor does the future expectation of an agreement establish definitive negotiation at that date.

### The $77 proposal and document submission

The target's 14 August letter requests both price and documents. Pfizer submits its markup on 18 August and its price on 19 August.

E11 route 1 expressly covers a response to a target request for both. The documents need not arrive in the same communication in that case.

The 18 August submission does not automatically create a $65 **Bid reaffirmed** row. E10 requires definitive negotiation to begin first.

The source does not report Medivation's selection of Pfizer for definitive negotiation before that submission. Pfizer's own authorization cannot supply that target decision.

The 18 August phone call repeats interest, not an earlier offer. It provides no separate Same-offer trigger.

If a workbook infers earlier definitive negotiation, inspect its precise target-side evidence. Do not reward an extra reaffirmation solely because a draft exists.

### Final financing language

The source gives two statements about the final transaction:

- Funds are available through sources that include cash on hand: p. 2, L451–455; p. 21, L1429–1431.
- “Pfizer has not entered into any financing commitment in connection with the Merger Agreement”: p. 21, L1431.

The final offer also has no financing condition. That fact appears at p. 2, L451 and in the offer introduction.

E12's amended rule gives **Contingent** precedence when the filing says the bid lacks a firm or committed financing arrangement, regardless of named sources.

If the absence of any financing commitment describes the final bid's financing state, **Contingent** and **Heavy/H1** follow that express priority.

A competing reading treats the passage as available internal funds with no need for external commitments. That supports **Not needed** under E12's cash rule.

The passage also describes the transaction at the filing date. Part B requires evidence of the state at the bid's communication, through the agreement.

These distinctions merit explicit review. The inventory does not force a final answer before a workbook's evidence window and rationale can be inspected.

It is a clear error to apply the later generic financing disclosure indiscriminately to all Pfizer bids. The 8 August proposal has its own express term.
