## Independent reconstruction

The seller marketed four selected stations, while keeping a broader LMG sale with a simultaneous NMG separation in view. From November 16, 2020, Moelis contacted 20 parties and 17 signed confidentiality agreements. A/B/C/D submitted station proposals; Gray/A/B also expressed LMG interest. Those five distinct bidders explain the 17 minus 5 equals 12 non-submitter residual, assuming the five are within the NDA population as the invitation sequence supports.

After December 21, the four station bidders received full station diligence access and the LMG parties received selected LMG information. January 12 solicited final station offers; January 25-26 brought five station-bidder submissions and two LMG proposals. The January 28 board paused the station path and carried Gray/B forward on LMG. D returned March 11 with a structurally different LMG transaction. March 17 solicited final LMG proposals from Gray/B; D subsequently received transaction drafts and a markup request. Gray/B bid April 14, D April 15. The April 16 price-improvement request to the same finalists is a defensible extension of the final LMG stage, not automatically another round.

Gray's LMG EV sequence is $2.5-2.7bn, $2.6-2.7bn, $2.45bn excluding MNI, $2.65bn, and $2.7bn. B's is $2.5-2.75bn, $2.5-2.7bn, $2.3bn including MNI, and $2.33bn. D's headline cash figures refinance debt as well as fund shareholder distributions, and are accompanied by changing retained equity and asset packages. These cannot be ranked as equivalent equity prices. The May 3 agreement pays $14.51 cash per share after a $1.975bn debt allocation and simultaneous NMG separation. D then competes after signing. Gray's $15.25, $16.99, $16.55, and restored $16.99 proposals change deal protections as well as price; June 2 signs the amendment and June 3 announces it.

Participation is internally reconcilable under the workbook's explicit assumptions: 17 NDA participants, 12 non-submitters, then A/C/D out January 28 leaves Gray/B; D's March return restores three. Treating B as out April 23 leaves Gray/D until signing. D's May 13 return is correctly separate from its pre-signing participation. Whether B's April 23 exit is early is addressed below. E's uncertain earlier NDA membership is not a reason to invent a further independent entry or exit.

## Material findings

### M1. The whole-company flag uses legal RemainCo scope instead of the economic scope Alex requires

**Class:** newer Alex guidance mismatch; C1 reference-company ambiguity. **Severity:** high. **Confidence:** high.

**Cells:** Deal facts B14; Questions C5/D5/F5 (Q4); Deal ledger E16:E18, E25, E28, E38, E45:E47, E51:E52, E54, E57:E58, E60:E61, E66, E68:E69, E72, E74:E77. These are the LMG proposals currently labeled `Bid`; station proposals already use `Other-scope bid`.

**Evidence:** P01040 p.59 specifies a "spin-off of NMG SpinCo ... with a simultaneous sale of LMG RemainCo". P00220 says the merger occurs "immediately after the consummation of the Spin-Off". Thus buying all stock of the surviving legal Meredith entity does not buy the pre-spin company's entire economic business. Alex V0097 says a deal without bids for the entire company should be flagged; V0095 says Meredith "cannot be a part of the estimation" but can remain useful for descriptive statistics. V0105 repeats that all the bids concern parts of the company.

**Assessment:** Q4 notices the issue but recommends `Yes`, and Deal facts B14 embeds that recommendation. This is not a factual failure to understand the legal merger; B2/B5 accurately describe the separation. It is a research-variable failure if `Whole-company bids=Yes` admits the deal to whole-company premium estimation. A Question exposing the issue does not repair the structured flag.

**Smallest correction/decision:** Record no bids for pre-spin Meredith as a whole, identify LMG/selected-station scope, and explicitly retain for descriptives while excluding the intended whole-company-premium estimation. Decide whether C1's `Other-scope bid` should apply to every LMG proposal or whether a separate economic-scope flag is required. Current C1 does not explicitly state whether "company" means the pre-spin traded business or the legal merger entity; this ambiguity helped produce the mismatch. Do not imply that changing a label supplies the missing segment market-price counterfactual.

### M2. Gray's January LMG Heavy condition is justified using hindsight and financing silence

**Class:** unsupported C14 reasoning; conditionality needs correction/adjudication. **Severity:** medium. **Confidence:** high on unsupported rationale, medium on replacement level.

**Cells:** Deal ledger L25/P25, event #24. Compare L24/P24, #23, the contemporaneous station proposal.

**Evidence:** P01061 p.61 applies to both Gray proposals: "subject to completion of certain due diligence items and agreement on acceptable contract terms." It says Gray's final LMG proposal would have no financing contingencies, but does not state that January financing was uncommitted. P25 invokes "substantive diligence still ahead (done by 04/14)" and "no commitment reported" to support Heavy. C14 says silence about financing establishes neither commitment nor its absence and requires the conditions of the bid when made.

**Consequence:** A meaningful early offer receives the strongest conditionality label without an identified substantive diligence period or stated financing contingency. The same quoted diligence formulation produces Light for Gray's station offer and Heavy for its LMG offer.

**Smallest correction:** Remove the financing-silence and retrospective-completion arguments. Reassess against the January evidence: Unclear is conservative unless "certain due diligence items" is accepted as limited diligence, in which case Light is possible. There is no need to change its Informal formality label.

### M3. An economically material April 13 structure revision is missing as an event

**Class:** confirmed omission under C2/C12. **Severity:** medium. **Confidence:** high.

**Cells:** missing row between Deal ledger #42 (row43, April11) and #43 (row44, April14); compare P38 and P47.

**Evidence:** P01106 p.66: Party D submitted a revised structure reflecting "the spin-off of NMG and the sale of LMG RemainCo"; Party E would contribute stations "in exchange for an ownership interest rather than selling the stations ... for cash." P01081 p.63 describes the original March11 LMG spin-off. This is an affirmative bidder proposal with a changed business combination and consideration to the station contributor, not a routine counsel drafting turn.

**Consequence:** The eventual Morris Trust structure is captured in #46 on April15, but the reader cannot locate the April13 switch or see when E became an equity contributor. That matters for package comparability and the group-versus-support decision in Q7.

**Smallest correction:** Add a structure-change proposal event dated April13, with unsupported numeric price cells empty; record the changed spin-off direction and E's cash-to-equity contribution. Do not invent a reaffirmed price from March11. If the schema treats unpriced structural revisions as `Other material event`, use that consistently and link the later priced offer.

## Convention decisions and bounded concerns

### M4. First final solicitation versus the earlier December diligence stage

**Class:** round-convention tension within C8, already flagged in Q1. **Severity if changed:** high for round assignments. **Confidence:** high on chronology; medium on mandatory split.

**Cells:** Deal ledger #19/#21 (G20/G22), Rounds C3/D3/H3, Questions C2/F2. The workbook opens R2 at the advancement after December21 and labels the entire stage Announced as final, although the first reported final-offer solicitation is January12 (P01054/P01057 pp.60-61). P01053 only grants access and advances bidders. C8 says the first final solicitation opens a round even with unchanged bidders, but also instructs scanning the whole stage for finality and preserving the smallest map. The earlier stage is not expressly described as final or definitive negotiation in December.

**Decision:** On the literal first-final-request rule, January12 opens another round, with the subsequent LMG-final stage renumbered. Alternatively, approve treating the December advancement as the start of the station-final stage and document that the later letter implements it. Do not silently use later final language to date an earlier final announcement. The workbook appropriately asks the question; this report does not count the alternative itself as a proven missing round. January28 may separately be the start of a coherent LMG stage; Q1 also identifies that alternative.

### M5. Party B's April 23 exclusion is plausible but not established by proposal rejection alone

**Class:** unresolved source/convention issue, Q9. **Severity:** medium. **Confidence:** high on uncertainty.

**Cells:** E56/N56/O56/P56/V56 (#55), Rounds J4, Questions C10. P01127 p.68 authorizes telling B its proposal is "not competitive" and expressly keeps developing D's proposal. It does not report an executed exclusivity agreement or an explicit statement ending B's participation. C16 says rejecting a proposal while a bidder continues is not an exit; later silence is not an exact exit date. The inference is reasonable because the board actively develops Gray/D and B never rebids, but it is not a reported exclusion.

**Decision:** Adjudicate April23 inferred exclusion against a signing fallback or closure at the April27 financing-support permission (C4). If retaining April23, explain why this board decision establishes the complete continuing set. The choice affects whether there are two or three live units late in the LMG stage, not the number of priced proposals actually received.

### M6. Party D/E/B group status and conditional deal-protection terms require economic interpretation

**Class:** unresolved conventions, not confirmed errors. **Cells:** D38/D47 and later D rows; Q7/Q9; L68/L72/L74:L77.

E contributes stations for equity, and B later contributes stations for warrants. That is more than a lender relationship but does not by itself prove a joint bidding unit. Current C4's support exception makes Financial for D defensible; a Mixed classification needs a deliberate definition of whether contributing operating assets and participating in the combined entity makes a co-acquirer. Do not create separate bidder entries merely because E/B appear in the package.

Gray's post-signing offers are correctly Formal, but Light describes financing/diligence readiness while the offers demand substantial deal protections (force-the-vote, discounted station option, break fees). Decide whether C14's "another material stated condition" includes these negotiated contractual demands. All are recorded in Notes; this is not a missing-terms finding.
