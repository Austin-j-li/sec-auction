# Pipeline research: evidence before coding

21 September 2026. Research and design only. No extraction instruction, workbook, runner, or checker was changed, and no extraction experiment was launched.

## Local verdict: instruction and architecture

**Yes to a small, explicitly approved instruction revision; no to treating every workbook error as a missing instruction.** My recommended architecture experiment is a fixed, evidence-first workflow, not open-ended debate. Retain the current four-sheet workbook as the researcher's interface, and use machine-readable evidence records and decision provenance internally.

The live instruction is already **8,421 words**. It explicitly prohibits unsupported exact counts (C3/C16), double first contacts (C9), financing conclusions from silence (C14), and quotation support that merely mentions a bidder (D). It requires other filing sections to be checked (A), material economic revisions to be captured (C12), and a source reread before delivery (E). Synacor's exact residual, Providence's repeated contact, Kraton's missed NDA, Penford's invented exclusive recipient, and Meredith's missing structure revision therefore do not each call for another special-case paragraph. They reveal failures to execute existing rules. Sources: [current instruction](../../../SEC_Deal_Ledger_Extraction_Instruction.md), [adjudicated review](README.md).

## What the current pipeline actually does

The workflow is not literally one model completion. The [runner](../../tools/sandbox/run_model.py) starts one tool-using CLI agent in a fresh filesystem sandbox, with a copied instruction and filing, input hashes, recorded model configuration, logs, a timeout, and a separate explicit revision mode. That agent chooses its own reading, reasoning and workbook-writing steps. The architectural limitation is that those responsibilities are not separated into independently checkable outputs. See `prepare`, `provider_command`, and `validate_workbook` in the runner.

The [checker](../../tools/check_lean.py) is currently v1.4. It validates structure, date/label/link consistency and contiguous quote occurrence, and explicitly performs no bidder-population arithmetic or substantive grading. Its passing result is therefore compatible with the reviewed errors. The [current handoff](../../HANDOFF.md) says Jev was removed after its local trial showed no demonstrated real workbook correction; that is a historical local trial report, not a new independent evaluation of every rejected flag. Reintroducing an undifferentiated model judge is not the proposed next step.

Preserve filesystem isolation, but do not call it network isolation: the runner explicitly shares networking for the provider connection. If technical enforcement of source-only access becomes a requirement, review provider egress and tool permissions separately. No outside-source access by these completed extraction runs is alleged here.

## Scope and conclusion

This note is an **architecture proposal**, not a finding that a new pipeline has improved SEC deal-ledger accuracy. The [eight-case review](README.md) found that the current workbooks often capture bids and prices while still giving unsupported exact dates, counts, bidder exits, or economic classifications. A structural checker passed those workbooks. The most useful next experiment is a small, source-indexed pipeline that separates **what the filing says**, **what follows from those facts under a stated rule**, and **what remains a research-convention choice**. Its first objective should be fewer unsupported exact claims in consequential fields, not a more elaborate agent conversation.

Two research results motivate this separation but do not validate it for merger filings. In long-context question answering and key-value retrieval, relevant material in the middle of a long context was harder for studied models to use than material near the ends; this is a reason to test indexed passage access against a full-filing prompt, not proof that chunking always wins [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172). FActScore's authors evaluated long-form biographies as atomic, source-supported facts; a ledger likewise contains many separately testable claims, but its economic coding and event completeness demand additional checks [Min et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251).

## Proposed minimal pipeline

1. **Freeze the permitted source and rules.** For each isolated run, record the filing and instruction hashes, model/runtime configuration, and a stable paragraph index. The extractors may see only that instruction and that filing. Search the entire permitted filing, including annexes relevant to dates, advisers, consideration, or agreement terms, while retaining the section and paragraph locator. Indexing is a navigation aid; every cited passage must be checked in the original filing context. Keep reference answers, older workbooks, case reviews, and human corrections outside the extraction sandbox. A later revision pass can receive a selected workbook and adjudicated findings only when explicitly authorized. This preserves the project's current isolation rule; it is a **design requirement**, not a claim from the papers below.

2. **Collect atomic source facts before assigning ledger codes.** One pass produces records such as: `actor as named`, `action`, `object/business`, `event date or interval`, `source paragraph`, `short evidence span`, `explicit/inferred`, and `alternatives`. A fact record describes the filing, for example "Company H said it remained interested but could not raise its range"; it does not silently convert that statement into "Company H exited on May 23." The source index should support both chronological reading and field-specific retrieval. This borrows the testable unit of analysis from FActScore, while the event vocabulary and evidence fields are **proposed for this ledger**, not results reported by that paper [Min et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251).

3. **Resolve identities and chronology explicitly.** Use a small set of event and actor tables keyed by source-local actor IDs, process IDs, and business scope; no graph database is needed. Represent `contacted`, `signed NDA`, `submitted indication`, `invited to final round`, `submitted final bid`, `withdrew`, and `transaction signed` as different relations. Dates may be exact, bounded, or unknown; do not replace a late-summer window with one artificial day. Alias resolution needs an evidence link or an unresolved candidate set. A cohort of 11 that includes already-recorded Party A contributes 10 *new* contacts, not 11. A cohort's undated NDA signing window cannot inherit a common exit date from a subset. These are **deterministic reconciliation rules proposed from the review's failure modes**, not model judgments.

4. **Apply the approved research conventions as a separate transform.** Map facts and participation intervals to rounds, bidder risk sets, bids, conditions, process boundaries, and whole-company scope. Preserve the rule ID, its inputs, and whether a value is direct, arithmetic, inferred, or adjudicated. For example, a completeness rule might infer an unadvanced bidder's closure at a final-round invitation; the filing's statement that the bidder was still invited to improve remains in the fact layer. Where these readings conflict, record both and route the choice to the researcher. This makes recalculation possible after a convention change without pretending that the new convention was in the filing.

5. **Emit typed candidate rows, then validate and reconcile.** A schema can require enums, nullable dates, evidence locators, and typed count bounds. JSON Schema's `required`, `enum`, and type constraints govern structure and permissible forms, but they cannot establish that "13 non-submitters" follows from the source or that a legal RemainCo is the right economic company [JSON Schema validation specification](https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12/draft-bhutton-json-schema-validation-00). Add deterministic checks for duplicate identity across established cohorts, inconsistent count arithmetic, and chronology. Checks depend on the correctness of the annotated facts: a markup-versus-bid deadline check needs an evidence-backed deadline-kind field, and a bid after an exit needs a supported re-entry or an unresolved-status finding, not automatic deletion. These checks should produce review findings and provenance; they should never silently rewrite a fact or impose a disputed economic convention.

6. **Verify claims source-first, with selective escalation.** Give a verifier the same permitted filing and rule set, ask it to locate relevant passages and reconstruct high-risk facts before showing it the candidate row. Then ask whether each atomic claim is supported, contradicted, underspecified, or requires a convention decision. This design aims to reduce anchoring on the candidate. Chain-of-Verification tested draft-specific verification questions answered independently and found lower hallucination rates on Wikidata lists, MultiSpanQA, and long-form generation; **source-first SEC verification is an extrapolation**, not a measured outcome of that study [Dhuliawala et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495). A verifier must also look for *missing* bids and events, since proving listed rows have citations does not measure recall.

Use a triage rule to spend the costly pass on fields that can change research conclusions: whole-company scope, process/round boundaries, bidder identity and live status, counts, bid price and conditions, and dates that govern participation. Also escalate contradictory passages, uncertain aliases, source silence paired with an exact claim, arithmetic/graph violations, and model disagreement. Low-risk descriptive notes can receive sampled audit. The risk ordering is a **project judgment** based on the eight-case review, not a published performance result.

## Abstention and ambiguity are outputs

The fact layer should distinguish `not reported`, `reported but ambiguous`, `inferred by rule`, and `explicitly stated`. A machine-readable `null` for an unsupported exact count or date should travel with a reason and, where available, bounds. For Synacor, the reviewed non-submitter count depends on unresolved group membership; storing an exact 13 alongside a Question is not a valid substitute for leaving the value unresolved. For Meredith, recording that the filing describes a simultaneous spin-off and sale does not itself settle which economic entity belongs in a whole-company premium sample. These examples come from the [local eight-case review](README.md); the proposed representation is new.

The reviewer must be allowed to return a short, actionable issue such as "source supports an interest statement, not an observed exit" or "two plausible rule interpretations change the round count." Abstention is not a generic low-confidence escape hatch: require the missing premise, candidate alternatives, and the affected field. Do not impute a numeric midpoint or an arbitrary date merely to satisfy workbook completeness. The export can retain blanks and an issue register; estimation code should exclude or bound affected observations according to an approved policy.

No amount of structured output, self-critique, debate, or extra model calls can determine the normative definition of whole-company scope, whether a first final solicitation always opens a new round, how to handle a process gap whose endpoints are not dated, how to treat an unadvanced but still-interested bidder, or how post-withdrawal renegotiation changes a deadline outcome. Those require an Austin/Alex decision and a versioned rule. Models can surface consequences and evidence for each option; they cannot turn a convention into an observed fact.

## Comparators and failure modes

| Approach | Likely value | Limit for this ledger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current single-agent filing-to-workbook workflow, then mechanical checker | Lowest orchestration overhead among these designs; already captures many prices/events in the reviewed cases. | A schema-valid row with a matching quotation can still assert an unsupported date or miscode an economic unit; missing events go unchecked. Actual token and human-review costs still need measurement. |
| Same answer plus ungrounded self-reflection | Low implementation cost. | In reasoning experiments, intrinsic self-correction without external feedback often failed and sometimes worsened answers; transfer to extraction is untested [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798). |
| Several samples with majority vote | May reduce sampling variance where a unique correct answer is recoverable. | Self-consistency improved arithmetic and commonsense benchmark results, but shared source omissions or a disputed convention can receive unanimous wrong votes [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171). |
| Free-form debate | Can expose a disputed claim. | Evidence is task-dependent: one debate study helped weak judges, while a hard reading-comprehension study found no human accuracy gain from two-turn debate [Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782), [Parrish et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10860). A debate transcript is not source proof. |
| Source facts, explicit transforms, targeted independent checks | Makes unsupported specificity and convention-sensitive outputs visible and testable. | More calls, indexing work, and reviewer time; retrieval gaps, alias errors, and correlated model errors remain possible. **No SEC-ledger improvement has yet been measured.** |

The staged design can fail by missing a passage during indexing, losing context around a quotation, merging two anonymous parties, treating an interval endpoint as an exact date, or accepting a verifier's plausible but source-free paraphrase. It can also overproduce flags that burden the human reviewer or let a retrieved passage from a later exhibit override an earlier event without temporal context. Mitigations are source locators back to full context, an explicit "unknown" state, deterministic graph checks, independent source sweeps, and selective human adjudication. None guarantees completeness.

Cache immutable parsing/indexing by filing hash and deterministic validation by input hash. Cache model outputs only with the full prompt, instruction version, model identifier, and source hash in the key; otherwise a rule revision could reuse stale decisions. Batch independent passage or field checks if the runtime supports it, while preserving one-filing/one-instruction isolation. Record prompt/input/output token counts, wall time, and human review minutes. These are **engineering proposals**; no universal cost reduction or current price is assumed.

## Controlled evaluation before adoption

Treat the reviewed eight deals as a regression set for known defects, not a clean accuracy benchmark. First freeze explicit convention decisions and build a small adjudicated claim set for those deals: named events, prices, bidder identities, dates/intervals, counts, scope, round boundaries, and deliberately unresolved cases. Record source spans and rationales. Then, only on a newly authorized set of unseen filings, compare paired isolated runs of (A) the current single-agent baseline, (B) source facts plus deterministic reconciliation, and (C) B plus targeted source-first verification. Hold the same instruction, filing, model configuration, and output schema fixed within the comparison except for the tested pipeline stage. Randomize run order and blind adjudicators to condition. Do not expose references or review findings to extracting agents.

Primary outcome: consequential unsupported or incorrect claims per deal and per eligible field, separately for scope, bidder participation, rounds, prices, dates, and conditions. Also measure event/actor recall from a human source sweep, exact-count/date overclaim rate, appropriate abstention rate, unresolved-but-exported exact values, and severity-weighted errors that alter the bidder risk set or estimation eligibility. Report inter-adjudicator disagreements rather than forcing false precision. Operational outcomes: tokens, runtime, reviewer minutes, number of escalations, and errors caught per review minute. Compare paired case-level differences with uncertainty intervals; do not advertise a single accuracy percentage from eight familiar cases.

Adopt a stage only if it reduces consequential errors on unseen cases without unacceptable loss of event recall or reviewer time. If B helps but C adds little, keep B. If a verifier merely increases flags without improving adjudicated outcomes, revise its triage or stop it. If a finding depends on a convention, send the researcher the source evidence and alternatives; do not score one convention as model error before the rule is set. This is a **prospective decision rule**, not a result.

## A concrete walkthrough: Synacor

The existing failure is not an inability to subtract 1 from 14. It is treating an uncertain set-membership premise as known. A proposed evidence record would preserve the reported 14 NDA parties, CLP's established membership, E/G's reported proposals and the unresolved E/G overlap with that NDA population. The coding layer would retain the review's conditional 11-13 non-submitter range and a blank exact Count, rather than choose 13 because a scalar is convenient. The number of NDA parties, the number of non-submitters and the number of live bidder units are separate quantities. Sources: [Synacor finding 1](synacor.md), [independent adjudication](adjudication.md).

The verifier would first answer from the filing: which submitting parties are established NDA-cohort members, and which memberships are unresolved? Only afterward would it inspect the candidate residual. A separate completeness sweep would look for omitted proposals, because checking only the candidate's evidence would miss a bidder absent from both its fact records and workbook. Human adjudication remains necessary where the source cannot resolve membership; the output should preserve that uncertainty rather than ask agents to vote it away.

## Minimal implementation experiment

The following design is a proposal for a later authorized experiment, not implemented code:

```text
Original filing + approved instruction
    -> source index and evidence records
    -> economic coding with explicit assumptions and rule references
    -> deterministic reconciliation + source-first independent review
    -> bounded correction and source check
    -> deterministic four-sheet XLSX export + evidence/issue sidecar
```

Keep intermediate records small: stable fact/event ID, source-local actor or anonymous cohort, economic object/scope, action, date bounds, exact count or count bounds, source locators, evidence status, and the rule/premises behind a derived value. Field-level evidence is important: one quote can establish a bid amount without supporting its date, conditions, or exit implication. The workbook's 30-word quotation remains a display excerpt; internal records can point to every supporting passage without putting all of them in a cell.

The semantic coding stage still needs model judgment. Only well-specified transformations, reconciliation and export should be ordinary code. A source index must preserve the full background and access to relevant annexes; top-k retrieval alone must not define what counts as the source. No vector database, agent framework, graph database, fine-tuning, or provider migration is a prerequisite for this experiment. Those would be separate choices justified only by measured need.

Use the existing runner's isolation and metadata conventions. Generate derived source indexes within the approved run, and retain an unmodified filing. Every stage sees only that filing, the approved instruction and outputs derived within the same run. The independent reviewer must not see reference answers, historical case reviews or the extractor's private rationale before its source reconstruction. A stage is independently prompted, not assumed statistically independent merely because it uses another model.

For token economy, start with one evidence/coding worker and one fresh targeted reviewer, not eight full rereads by eight models. Review high-consequence fields and run a source-only omission sweep; sample lower-risk unflagged claims so the routing system's misses remain visible. Cache deterministic parsing, reuse stable context where supported, and keep one bounded correction cycle before escalating unresolved findings. Source passages, not confidence scores alone, must justify every correction. A changed fact invalidates dependent counts/rounds; verify those dependencies and confirm that correct prices were preserved. The budget and one-cycle stopping rule are proposed controls, not empirically optimal settings.

This is a fixed workflow with limited routing and verification. Anthropic's first-party engineering guidance distinguishes predefined workflows from agents that choose their own execution path and describes chaining, routing and evaluator-optimizer patterns. That is useful architectural vocabulary, not evidence of a particular SEC accuracy gain; its 2024 article also warns that some tooling descriptions have since changed [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents).

## Recommended sequence and stopping rules

1. **Resolve conventions, then draft a narrow instruction change.** Approve the scope/round/participation/deadline decisions first. Add small generic counterexamples outside any unseen evaluation set. Do not teach case-specific Alex answers to a blind extraction. Success means a researcher can specify the expected output and deliberate unknowns consistently; unresolved policy is not an extraction failure.
2. **Separate instruction testing from architecture testing.** First check an approved instruction revision using the existing architecture. In the architecture comparison, hold the approved instruction, source and model settings fixed. Do not attribute a changed economic definition to a better model or pipeline.
3. **Test the smallest addition before the complete redesign.** Compare the single-agent baseline with that same baseline plus one source-first reviewer and bounded correction. Then test evidence-first typed records, reconciliation and deterministic export; finally test whether the extra verifier helps that version. Treat the reviewed eight cases as regressions, and reserve newly authorized, unseen filings for the decision to adopt.
4. **Measure both gains and collateral damage.** Human adjudicators, blind to condition, should compare errors removed, errors introduced, omitted material events, unsupported exactness, correct versus unnecessary abstention, and token/latency/reviewer cost. Use source-derived event/claim units, not the number of model-generated rows, as denominators where possible. Review a sample of unflagged claims and repeat a subset of runs to reveal variance. A small pilot cannot establish a precise generalization rate.
5. **Keep only the stages that earn their cost.** Stop a verifier that mainly creates false alarms or new errors. Retain the simpler architecture if it performs as well. Do not revise until every model agrees; remaining factual uncertainty is an acceptable result, and remaining economic convention disputes return to Austin/Alex. Match the acceptable error and reviewer-time thresholds to the intended research use before seeing pilot results.

The use of code checks, model review and human calibration is also consistent with Anthropic's 2026 first-party evaluation guidance, which distinguishes regression tests from capability tests and notes that model-based graders require calibration [Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). This note's specific experimental arms and stopping rules are our proposal.

No instruction change, new extraction, paid model experiment, canonical workbook revision, or implementation has been authorized by this research discussion. The next decision is whether to approve the narrow convention/wording proposal and then a bounded pilot, not whether to replace the whole pipeline immediately.
