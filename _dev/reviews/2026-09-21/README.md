# SEC extraction quality review

## Verdict

The eight v1.11 workbooks are useful research drafts, but the batch is **not ready for unattended estimation**. Most disclosed bid prices and major transaction events are captured well. Remaining errors concentrate in the variables that matter most for this project: which bidders are live, when they leave, what constitutes a round, what business is being acquired, and what the bidder knew or still needed to do.

PetSmart and Penford are the strongest cases and need narrow corrections. Providence & Worcester, Mac-Gray, Kraton, sTec and Synacor are usable after targeted corrections and explicit decisions on the remaining conventions. Meredith is useful as a descriptive record after corrections, but **should be excluded from the intended whole-company premium estimation under Alex's newer guidance**. A simultaneous spin-off means the acquired business has no separately observed unaffected market price in this filing. Relabeling the offers does not solve that economic measurement problem.

This is a substantive model-assisted review, not human acceptance or a fresh extraction. Six subagents, three Astra and three Sol, reviewed the cases, audited Alex's reference provenance, and independently challenged selected material findings. The root checked the key source passages and reconciled those judgments. No workbook, filing, reference document or extraction instruction was changed.

## The most consequential findings

1. **Meredith's structured scope flag answers the wrong economic question.** `Deal facts!B14=Yes` and the LMG `Bid` labels treat acquisition of all stock of legal RemainCo as a whole-company acquisition. The filing describes a simultaneous NMG spin-off and LMG sale. Alex V0097 specifically calls these partial-company offers; V0095 says to retain the deal for descriptives but exclude it from estimation. The workbook notices the issue in Q4, then recommends the legal-entity interpretation. This is an economic-scope mismatch, not a failure to notice the spin-off. The supplied C1 does not fully define the reference company around a simultaneous carve-out. See [Meredith](meredith.md), M1, and [independent adjudication](adjudication.md), section 1.

2. **Several exact participation statements exceed what the source identifies.** Synacor `Deal ledger!M41=13` non-submitters is conditional on G and E being outside the 14 NDA signers; their membership is unresolved, and G did submit a segment offer to outreach that covered the company and/or that segment. Leave the exit count blank and preserve the conditional bounds; do not merely replace 13 with 12. Mac-Gray's exact total of 16 unnamed financial signers is supported, but its common July 25 exit is not: their signing window extends beyond that date. Those are different errors, one in residual membership and one in event timing. See [Synacor](synacor.md), finding 1; [Mac-Gray](mac-gray.md), finding 1; adjudication sections 5-6.

3. **sTec turns a bidder statement into an observed target exit.** Company H's May 23 statement says it remains interested but cannot raise its range. `Deal ledger!B40/E40/O40` records an exact, non-inferred target exclusion that day. The narrow correction is certain: the filing does not report that exit. Under the current complete-advancing-set rule, the preferred coding is an inferred closure by May 16, when WDC and D receive final-round letters. Whether H should remain economically live while invited to improve is a convention decision. Separately, WDC's May 28/30 `Light` conditions rely on the hoped-for June 3 signing date being interpreted as expedited diligence. The source does not establish that; `Unclear` is better supported than automatic `Light` or `Heavy`. See [sTec](stec.md), S1-S2, and adjudication sections 3-4.

4. **Providence repeats Party A in the first-contact population.** Party A has a first-contact row with Count 1, and the subsequent strategic Contact cohort includes it again in Count 11. In the workbook's one continuous process, current C9 requires 10 new strategic contacts plus the already-recorded A, preserving the filing's 11-person wave in the Note. This is a one-party inflation in derived unique-contact counts, not an error in the 25-person initial NDA total. See [Providence & Worcester](providence-worcester.md), finding 1.

5. **Kraton misses a fact that the filing resolves outside the background.** Annex A-4 dates Parent's confidentiality agreement to June 3, 2021, while Q3 says no party is named as a signer. Split that signer out of the six-strategic cohort and reduce the residual to five; do not add another NDA party. The September 8 markup due date is also coded as a bid deadline even though final prices were due September 15. Further narrow corrections concern the post-Reuters signer's signing closure and Party J's exit-round consistency. See [Kraton](kraton.md), K1-K4.

6. **Even the stronger cases need source-precision edits.** Penford's `Deal ledger!P42` says adjusted projections went *only* to Deutsche Bank; the filing names Deutsche Bank but does not exclude other recipients. Its August 21 presentation note also should not imply delivery of a version updated on August 28. PetSmart's nine non-submitters are a correct arithmetic residual but need the inferred flag and inferred-date wording. Its buyer counsel is identifiable in the agreement notices but omitted. These corrections preserve the core price and auction histories. See [Penford](penford.md) and [PetSmart](petsmart.md).

Other confirmed findings are recorded in the case reports, including Meredith's omitted April 13 economic-structure revision and unsupported rationale for Gray's January Heavy conditions, and Synacor's miscoding of Company E's refusal to return to a 100% acquisition as refusal to improve its price.

## Decisions that should not be disguised as factual repairs

**First final solicitation versus a continuing diligence stage.** Meredith starts a station-diligence round after December 21 and records the first final-offer request on January 12 only as a deadline event. Kraton starts a stage July 20 and records its first final procedures letter after August 11 only as a deadline event. Literal C8 says the first final request opens a round even if bidders are unchanged, which favors an additional round in each case. The competing reading treats the letter as implementing the existing coherent stage. Both workbooks expose the alternative. The explicit trigger should govern unless Austin/Alex deliberately clarify that convention; neither model agreement nor the banker's stage numbering settles the economic definition.

**Processes and continued participation.** sTec's one-process map differs from Alex's explicit two-process reading, but its last 2012 acquirer contact is undated, making C7's gap threshold uncertain. Synacor correctly separates the long 2019-to-2020 gap and preserves October 2020 continuity; the earlier 2018-to-2019 boundary is less firmly dated. sTec H and some non-admitted Penford bidders also expose tension between mechanical advancement closure and a continuing opportunity to bid. These decisions affect the sample's process count and bidder risk sets.

**Conditions and deadline outcomes.** A returned markup can support Formal without proving None. Providence's August 4 Party B `Light` code is better supported under current rules than the voice note's unconditional description because diligence continued. Penford's October 8 returned draft leaves a genuine condition-level question. sTec's later accepted WDC bids in the same round create tension with labeling the May 30 deadline Enforced; a rule for renegotiation after withdrawal may be needed. Preserve source uncertainty about whether an original due date was reached before an undated extension.

**Cash earnouts and bidder groups.** Kraton preserves the earnout terms but uses `Not stated` for the cash flag; Alex calls it cash plus a contingent cash payment, while the filing does not explicitly say the base is cash. PetSmart correctly separates Longview's rollover from a consortium, but the identities of both members of Bidder 3 remain uncertain. These should remain explicit decisions, not invented identities or consideration facts.

## What is working well

The workbooks implement many of the specific fixes Alex requested. Examples include PetSmart's otherwise easy-to-miss third initially high-valuing bidder and final $82.50-to-$83 revision; sTec's $9.15 withdrawal and return at $6.60-$7.10 before $6.85; Meredith's alternative structures and $16.99-to-$16.55-to-$16.99 reversal; the distinction between confidentiality signers and contacts; formal bids with separately recorded conditions; rollover support without a fabricated consortium; and separate signing and announcement events. sTec also captures the June 19 projections and Wells Fargo relationship outside the background.

Reference disagreement is not itself an error. Mac-Gray's filing supports September 24 exits for A and B, while Alex's newer voice note names B and C; C had already failed to answer the final solicitation. The current ledger is right on that point. Old Meredith and Kraton entries contain price/date errors that the new ledgers avoid. Penford correctly treats Party A's earlier valuation statements as distinct from a submitted offer. These counterexamples are why the review tested claims against the filing instead of grading agreement with the old workbook.

The Questions sheets are valuable: they expose many of the actual research choices. Their weakness is that some rows still store a single exact number, date or classification that the accompanying Question admits is unsupported. A question flag does not make that exact value reliable for downstream code.

## Reference provenance and review scope

Five cases have Alex-personally-augmented older records: Providence & Worcester, Mac-Gray, PetSmart, Penford and sTec. Kraton, Meredith and Synacor have substantive inherited Chicago RA rows, not Alex hand-extracted benchmarks. All eight have later direct Alex voice commentary. The March 3 reference memo's nine-case list and the older workbook's red edits corroborate this distinction. The newer voice document also contains a clearly labeled Claude summary of an earlier dataset; its historical counts and error percentages were not used to score the current run. Full provenance and conflicts are in [the reference audit](alex_reference_audit.md).

The review covered all eight current workbooks: **473 ledger events, 25 Rounds entries and 59 Questions**, plus every Deal facts entry. Each primary case reviewer read the complete relevant filing background and checked relevant other sections for types, dates, advisers, conditions, consideration and information. They compared the appropriate reference rows and complete case voice sections. Astra reviewed sTec/PetSmart and Meredith/Kraton; Sol reviewed Mac-Gray/Providence and Penford/Synacor. A separate Sol agent audited reference provenance; a separate Astra agent challenged six selected material findings. The root inspected the source evidence for the main conclusions and reconciled the reports. This was not two complete independent reviews of every case.

The saved mechanical reports have **zero errors and 101 length warnings**. Their reuse was justified by exact SHA-256 equality of the reviewed workbooks, the checked run outputs and the original validation records. All reviewed source hashes were checked before and after the review. The mechanical checks establish structure and quotation occurrence, not semantic correctness or completeness. The verification record is [input_verification.json](input_verification.json).

## Recommended next step

Use a targeted correction pass, followed by source checking of changed cells and a reconciliation of affected bidder counts and round assignments. Preserve the correct price histories. First fix unsupported facts and exact counts; then decide the process, round, scope and conditionality conventions; only then derive an estimation dataset. Meredith should remain outside the intended whole-company-premium sample. A wholesale blind rerun is not needed to act on the documented corrections and would not resolve unsettled conventions.

This review proposes corrections; it does not apply them. The remaining questions are concrete: which economic entity defines whole-company scope, whether a first final letter always opens a fresh round, how uncertain contact gaps are treated, when an unadvanced but still-invited bidder ceases to be live, and whether post-withdrawal renegotiation changes an earlier deadline's outcome.

## Validation boundary

No accuracy percentage, precision/recall estimate or causal claim is warranted. There is no complete, independently adjudicated gold standard, and source silence prevents exact reconstruction of some private decisions. This audit identifies concrete, reviewable defects and defensible alternatives; it cannot guarantee the absence of omissions. The reviewed eight deals are familiar examples, not a clean unseen test of v1.11 generalization. No external bidder identification, market-price reconstruction or other web evidence was used.

The individual reports preserve cell coordinates, source page/paragraph references, short quotations, relevant voice/reference cells, consequences and corrections. Their paragraph labels can be regenerated from the hash-locked local sources with `prepare_evidence.py`; `verify_inputs.py` records byte identity and coverage. This Markdown index and its linked case reports are the primary handoff. The HTML companion combines the same evidence in one file, but remains a preview because its interaction checks did not pass; see [report validation notes](REPORT_METHOD.md).
