## How the sale went
Datalink agreed to be bought by Insight Enterprises for $11.25 per share in cash, signed on 11/06/2016 and announced on 11/07/2016.
- **Round 0 and round 1:** In January 2016 a strategic buyer, Party A, made an unsolicited $10.00 cash offer. Datalink negotiated with it one-on-one and asked for a written proposal. Party A offered $10.30–$10.75 but needed financing, and the board turned it down "at that time" on 04/20.
- **Round 2:** From 06/06 Raymond James contacted 27 potential buyers, and 23 signed confidentiality agreements. The board considered ten initial offers: nine new ones plus Party A's March proposal.
- **Round 3:** The board advanced the five highest offers: Insight, Party B, Party C and two unnamed parties.
- **Round 4:** Only Insight, Party B and Party C submitted final proposals. Insight's arrived a day after the 08/30 deadline. After the target pushed for improvements, Insight reached $12.00 and got 30 days of exclusivity on 09/02. The two unnamed parties eventually decided not to submit.
- **Round 5:** Insight then demanded accounting concessions before it would sign, so the board let exclusivity lapse and went back to Party B and Party C on 10/01. Party B withdrew on 10/24 after seeing the third-quarter results. Party C stayed at $11.25 and was not shown those results. Insight got exclusivity again on 10/26, cut its price to $11.00 on 11/01, and agreed to $11.25 when Datalink countered.

## Questions and my recommendations
1. **Process and round map:** one process. The pre-June talks with Party A are a solicited round 1, because Datalink asked for its written proposal. The re-approach after exclusivity lapsed is an inferred round 5. The Question gives the most plausible alternative for each boundary. There is no gap of two months or more without contact with a buyer.
2. **Reaffirmations:** Party A's 04/12 and Party C's 10/04 reaffirmations are recorded as bids. Insight's 10/26 confirmation of $12.00 gets no reaffirmed-bid row, because its $12.00 bid is already formal and in the final round.
3. **Party A after July:** the filing doesn't say whether Party A was among the five advanced. It stays inside the unnamed groups rather than getting its own exit.
4. **Round 2 deadlines:** both marked Enforced.
5. **Round 2 non-submitter counts (5 strategic, 8 financial):** these are exact only if every unnamed initial bidder had signed a confidentiality agreement. I recommend keeping them, marked as inferred.
6. **The two unnamed finalists:** I kept them in round 4, with the filing's reported "decided not to submit" exit, even though the final-proposal letter went only to the other three.
7. **Conditions:** all final-round bids are Heavy, because each needed a stated 30-day (Party C: 30–45-day) exclusivity period before signing. Insight's November bids are Light.
8. **Round 4 deadline:** Late bids accepted, because Insight's proposal came a day late and was still considered.
9. **Inferred drops:** Party B and Party C are treated as dropped when Insight got exclusivity on 09/02, and as re-entering on 10/01. Party C is dropped again at the 10/26 exclusivity. This follows the instruction's rule, though the board clearly still had Party C in mind in November.
10. **Party B's exit reason:** "Other stated reason" rather than "Value below earlier offer".

## What I could not pin down
- **Dates:** the filing gives no dates for when the confidentiality agreements were signed, when the round 2 offers arrived, or when the two unnamed finalists decided not to submit. Those rows carry only the bounds the filing supports.
- **Prices:** no individual prices are given for the six unnamed initial offers. Only their group range, $8.80–$10.50, is recorded, on one row covering all six.
