# Datalink: the sale and the round-boundary choice

Datalink ultimately agreed to be acquired by Insight for **$11.25 per share in cash**. The agreement was signed November 6, 2016 and announced November 7. Before that, Datalink moved from negotiations with one interested buyer into a broader competitive sale. The question is how to label the earlier bilateral stage, not whether those negotiations happened.

All facts below come from the [November 29 proxy filing](../../../raw_filing/datalink_2016-11-29_DEFM14A.htm), mainly its background on printed pages 27–35. The anonymous names are the filing's names; their identities were not inferred.

| Period | What happened | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| January–April | Party A approached Datalink and offered $10 cash on January 19. On January 29, the board decided management should pursue the opportunity. An NDA followed on February 4. Datalink requested a written proposal in mid-March; A offered $10.30, potentially up to $10.75 if financial criteria were met, on March 29, with financing still a condition. | This developed beyond an unsolicited approach into an organized negotiation with a requested offer. |
| April–June | On April 20 the board declined to pursue A's proposal at that time, but would consider a higher price backed by financing commitments. Conversations continued into May. The board then authorized a broader sale effort, and outreach began June 6, including A. | There was no clean termination and fresh start; A was kept in view while the buyer pool expanded. |
| June–July | The banker contacted 13 strategic acquirers and 14 financial sponsors; 23 signed NDAs. The board considered nine new initial proposals plus A's standing March proposal and selected the five highest-priced proposals on July 27. | This is the broad auction. The old A proposal and the new proposals must remain distinguishable. |
| August–September 2 | Three parties were expressly asked for final proposals. B offered $12, C $11, and Insight $11.75. After feedback C increased to $11.25 and Insight to $12. Datalink preferred Insight's route to closing over B's financing structure and third-party diligence requirements, and over C's price/financing. Insight received exclusivity on September 2 through September 30. | Competition included price and execution risk. A revised price within this final stage does not automatically open another round. The two other July advancers were still considering proposals on September 1 and ultimately did not submit. |
| Late September–October | Insight insisted on additional pre-closing accounting and auditor requirements. Datalink let exclusivity expire and re-engaged B and C while continuing discussions with Insight. B later withdrew after reviewing updated financial information; C maintained its $11.25 offer. Datalink granted Insight exclusivity again on October 26. | Rival participation changed again. A temporary exclusive negotiation did not mean the whole sale process had ended. |
| November | After reviewing weaker results, Insight reduced its offer to $11 on November 1. Datalink countered at $11.25, Insight agreed November 2, and the parties signed November 6. | The final sale price was below the $12 reached during the earlier competitive stage. |

A **process** is the continuing attempt to sell the company. A **round** is a stage organized by the target: who is invited, what they must submit and the terms of that solicitation. Another offer or another request to improve price does not by itself create a round.

The raw workbook uses one process and five rounds: January bilateral negotiations; June broad outreach; July selection of five for further diligence; August final proposals; October re-engagement after exclusivity lapsed. Round 0 is the preliminary bucket for approaches before an organized stage.

## The choice

1. **Keep January's bilateral stage as round 1 — recommended.** The board took up A's approach, allowed confidential access and expressly requested the March offer. June then becomes round 2 because the field expands. The January 29 opening remains marked as an inference.
2. **Start round 1 at the June auction.** Put January–May activity in round 0, then renumber the remaining stages. This follows a stricter reading of E6's priority for the first outreach wave.

The difference affects whether the research model treats A's March offer as a response to a bidding stage or as preliminary activity. It does not change the offer amounts, the nine new July proposals, the three final bidders, or the final sale price. My recommendation is the first reading because the target itself requested the March offer and continued to keep A in view.

After requesting this context, **Austin agreed on 21 September to keep January's bilateral stage as round 1**. June remains round 2 and the five-round map is retained; January 29 remains an inferred opening date. No workbook revision has been performed.
