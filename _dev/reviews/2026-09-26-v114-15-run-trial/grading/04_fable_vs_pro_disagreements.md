## 1. Issue by issue

### Issue 1. Mac-Gray: closing the 16 anonymous financial NDA signers

**Filing (p. 32).** "Over the next two months a total of 20 potential bidders, including two strategic bidders (Party A and CSC/Pamplona) and 18 financial bidders (including Party B and Party C), entered into confidentiality agreements … Parties that had entered into confidentiality and standstill agreements with Mac-Gray were furnished an informational package … which they were instructed to submit by July 23, 2013." The two months run from 24 June to about 24 August. **Party A, one of the 20, did not sign until 5 August** (p. 34: on 25 July it had only "agreed to enter into a confidentiality agreement"). So the NDA window demonstrably extends past the 23 July deadline. On 25 July the four named bidders advanced (p. 34). On 27 August the letter for revised proposals went to exactly those four (p. 35), after the whole NDA window had closed.

**Rules.** E3: "An NDA signed at some point in an interval does not prove eligibility at a deadline inside that interval." E3 Counts: "Never get an exact number of non-submitters by subtracting bids from a group that was not, as a whole, eligible at that deadline." E14's first transition, Did not submit, applies to a party "eligible for a solicitation". The second, Dropped by target, applies to a party "live in a stage" when "the complete advancing set is named or counted without it".

**What the workbooks wrote.**

| Workbook | Row | Label, date, count |
|---|---|---|
| Astra high (A) | #33 | Dropped by target, by 27 Aug, Count 16, Inferred; Note says some may date to 25 July |
| Sol xhigh (C) | #33 | Dropped by target, by 27 Aug, Count 16, Inferred |
| Astra xhigh (D) | #42 | Dropped by target, by 11 Sept, Count 16, Inferred |
| Opus medium (B) | #24 | Did not submit, by 23 July, Count blank, "up to 16 missed the deadline"; its own Q2 says Party A signed 5 Aug so dates spread past 23 July |
| Opus xhigh (E) | #23 | Did not submit, by 23 July, **Count 16 exact**; Note says eligibility at 23 July "is not established for each" |

**Pro.** Scored Opus medium 82 and Opus xhigh 80, mainly for this row: a dated failure-to-bid for a group not shown eligible; "a substantive participation error".

**Fable (in `02` §6).** Read p. 32 as making every signer eligible and so did not penalize Opus medium; penalized Opus xhigh only for the exact count.

**Verdict: Pro is right, and Fable's earlier reading was wrong.** The p. 32 instruction applies only to parties that had already signed; Party A shows that some of the 20 had not. Both Opus workbooks say so in their own Notes and still code the whole residual as July non-submitters, which is the exact situation E3 warns against. A 27 August bound, the first date by which every member is certainly closed, is the supported coding. Opus medium's blank Count softens the error but does not fix the date or the round-1 live count. The effect is on the round-1 live bidder count, a core research variable. On Fable's scale this is a cohort-level convention error: C 1.0 for each Opus workbook (Opus medium was 0; Opus xhigh was 0.5). Astra xhigh's 11 September is one stage too late, as both reviews already said.

**What the instruction should say** (sharpens `03` A1). Where a cohort's members entered over an interval that straddles a deadline, do not close them at that deadline; close the cohort at the first transition that applies to every member, with the earlier possibility in the Note.

### Issue 2. Providence & Worcester: the label for the non-IOI signers

**Filing.** p. 28: in the week of 28 March the banker contacted 11 strategic and 18 financial buyers; "Each of the potential strategic buyers and 14 potential financial buyers subsequently executed confidentiality agreements." The memorandum went to "the potential buyers, including Party A, Party B and G&W, that had executed confidentiality agreements", before 22 April. p. 29: on 27 April the board noted "that each potential buyer had been advised to submit a non-binding indication of interest by May 10, 2016", later postponed to 19 May. Nine IOIs arrived 19 May–1 June; two low bidders were excluded; seven advanced on 1 June.

**Rule.** E14: eligible, no submission reported, not mentioned again → **Did not submit** by the due date, with a bound where size is uncertain.

**What the workbooks wrote.**

| Workbook | Row | Label, date, count |
|---|---|---|
| Sol xhigh (A) | #17 | Did not submit, by 1 June, Count blank, "at least 16" |
| Astra high (B) | #18 | **Dropped by target**, by 1 June, Count blank, "16–24" |
| Astra xhigh (C) | #18 | **Dropped by target**, by 1 June, **Count 16** |
| Opus xhigh (D) | #20 | Did not submit, by 19 May, Count blank, "at least 16; 16–18 on recorded entries" |
| Opus medium (E) | #18 | Did not submit, by 1 June, Count blank, "at least 16" |

**Pro.** Praised Astra high's row as "the strongest treatment of uncertainty"; penalized the Did not submit rows because "eligibility not established". Pro did not discuss the label.

**Fable.** Penalized the Dropped by target label (C 1.0 each for Astra high and xhigh).

**Verdict: Fable is right on the label.** Unlike Mac-Gray, every initial signer had signed before the memorandum went out in April, and the filing says each potential buyer was advised of the deadline. Eligibility is established, so Did not submit is E14's first transition. "Dropped by target" says the company excluded them, which the filing never reports. Pro's eligibility objection does not carry over from Mac-Gray. The dates are secondary: 19 May is the due date; 1 June is the last day IOIs were accepted, and either works as a "by" date.

### Issue 3. Providence & Worcester: how big the residual is

**Filing.** The 11 + 14 = 25 initial signers (p. 28). Party B was introduced on 21 April as "another potential strategic buyer", so it may be outside the 11. G&W's NDA is dated 3 April (Annex p. A-48), right after the 28 March outreach, and G&W was among the five strategic buyers met at the 3–6 April convention. Party C signed in July. p. 33 says "25 of whom entered into confidentiality agreements", which cannot count all of these unless some overlap.

**The competing bounds.** Astra high: 16–24, on the logic that anywhere from one to nine of the nine IOI bidders might be original-cohort signers. Fable objected that the top end is not honest because only NDA signers had the memorandum. Opus xhigh: 16–18, allowing for G&W and Party B being extra. Others: "at least 16".

**Verdict: split.** Fable's objection was too quick: memorandum access shows that the IOI bidders were signers, not that they were among the original 25; Party B plausibly was not. So the lower end is 16 and the upper end is not fixed by the text. But an upper end of 24 needs eight of the nine IOI bidders to come from outside the original 25, and the filing names at most two candidates (Party B and possibly G&W). Opus xhigh's 16–18, tied to named parties, is the most defensible bound; "at least 16" is acceptable; 16–24 is honest but loose. Fable's K 0.25 on Astra high's range should be withdrawn. Astra xhigh's exact 16 is the only coding that overstates certainty.

### Issue 4. Providence & Worcester: G&W and Party B entered beside the 11-strategic cohort

**Filing.** As in issue 3. The filing never says whether G&W or Party B is one of "each of the potential strategic buyers" who signed.

**Rules.** E3: "A later-named party belongs to an earlier cohort only if the filing establishes it; if it does, it stays inside that cohort's Count and is not entered again." Also E3: "Where rows and total cannot be reconciled, or membership is uncertain, say so in the Note and raise a Question." Part F check 2: "Every stated population total reconciles to its rows … with no party counted twice."

**What Opus xhigh (D) wrote.** #9 cohort of 11 strategic signers; #11 G&W NDA (3 April) and #16 Party B NDA entered separately, each Note saying it "may be among the 11". Q3 recommends keeping them separate and reads p. 33's 25 as the first-wave total, giving at least 26–28 entries. The alternative (both inside the 11) is spelled out with its row effects. The other four workbooks kept G&W and Party B inside the 25.

**Pro.** Scored D lowest of the fifteen (78), with this as the main reason: the rows "cannot safely be summed as unique entrants".

**Fable.** Called it a literal application of E3 and deducted 0.5.

**Verdict: Pro is right on weight; Fable's deduction was too light.** D did follow E3's literal words, so this is partly an instruction problem. But G&W's 3 April NDA and its presence at the convention make it very likely one of the 11, and D's reading overrides an explicit filing total (25) to create 28 entries. That inflates the entry and live counts, the variables the ledger exists for. Fable's scale treats a cohort-level count error as C 1.0, not 0.5. This, with issues 7 and 9, is why Pro ranked D last on P&W, and that ordering is defensible.

**Instruction problem** (adds to `03` A8). E3's "only if the filing establishes it" should yield when entering the party separately would exceed a stated total: keep a named party inside the cohort, name the uncertainty in the Note, and raise a Question.

### Issue 5. Mac-Gray: $21.25 carried into the October commitment revisions

**Filing (p. 39).** 5 October: Kirkland (CSC/Pamplona's counsel) delivered a revised draft of the Pamplona commitment letter proposing that Pamplona's liability for damages "would be limited to an unspecified dollar amount". 8 October: Kirkland proposed, "at the direction of CSC/Pamplona", that Pamplona's liability "be capped at $50 million". Neither passage mentions price.

**Rule.** E10: "On a same-price revision the price cells hold the earlier price only where the filing shows it unchanged, noted like any carried term; otherwise they stay blank." Financing likewise needs express incorporation.

**What the workbooks wrote.** All five record these as Bid rows (correct under E10). Opus medium (#55, #57) and Opus xhigh (#53–54) fill $21.25 and Committed; Opus xhigh marks the price Inferred. Astra high, Astra xhigh and Sol leave the price blank.

**Pro.** Penalized the Opus carry-forward: it "should not become an additional observed price communication".

**Fable.** Listed it as a cross-deal difference and did not deduct.

**Verdict: Pro is right; Fable missed it.** E10's text is explicit, and the filing does not show the price in these communications. A downstream user who counts priced Bid rows would see two extra $21.25 observations. C 0.5 for each Opus workbook.

### Issue 6. Mac-Gray: Party A's 18 September "best and final"

**Filing (pp. 35–36).** 10 September: Party A's revised IOI at $18–19, "to be financed by outstanding credit facilities or affiliated equity sources", with no firm financing commitment. 18 September: "Party A reiterated in a telephone call … that its previous indication of interest with an all-cash purchase price of $18.00 to $19.00 per share was its best and final offer."

**Rule.** E10 express incorporation: carry a term "only where the filing says it was carried, and only within the scope stated".

**What the workbooks wrote.** Astra high, Opus medium and Opus xhigh carry Financing = Contingent and Heavy (H1), noting "Terms: as #n". Sol (#45) and Astra xhigh (#46) set Financing Not stated and Conditions Unclear.

**Pro.** Penalized Sol and Astra xhigh: the bidder reiterated its previous indication, not merely a price, so its uncommitted financing should stay visible.

**Fable.** Did not deduct either way.

**Verdict: Pro is right.** The bidder reiterated the whole previous indication, and that is an express statement of carry. Leaving financing Not stated makes A look better funded on 18 September than on 10 September, which is an artificial improvement. C 0.5 for Sol and Astra xhigh.

### Issue 7. Providence & Worcester: Party E's 60-day exclusivity as H2

**Filing (p. 30).** Party E's LOI at $21.26 was "subject to a 60-day exclusivity period for due diligence and negotiation of definitive documentation".

**Rule.** E12: "For H2, an exclusivity period, the time to signing or a negotiation period is not a diligence period." The examples table: "A request for several weeks of exclusivity; no stated diligence period → … normally Unclear."

**What the workbooks wrote.** Opus xhigh (#30; and #49 on E's later bid) codes Heavy (H2), flagged in Questions. The other four code Unclear.

**Pro.** Penalized: the instruction "expressly distinguishes an exclusivity period from a stated diligence period".

**Fable.** Grouped it with the H2 gray zone (`03` A2) and did not deduct.

**Verdict: Pro is right.** The period combines exclusivity, diligence and documents, and E12 says a period of that kind is not a diligence period. The words "for due diligence" make it arguable, which is why Opus xhigh flagged it, but the rule text points to Unclear. Fable deducted Sol 0.5 for an unflagged H2 on Mac-Gray and should have been consistent: C 0.5 for Opus xhigh here.

### Issue 8. Mac-Gray: Light on CSC's 18 September bid

**Filing.** CSC's 18 September proposal (p. 36) restates Pamplona's full capital commitment and asks for exclusivity; it says nothing about remaining diligence. The "confirmatory business and legal due diligence" passage (p. 38) covers 25 September to 7 October, after exclusivity. The earlier "follow-up and confirmatory" calls (27 August–3 September, p. 35) were with Party B, Party C and Party A, not CSC.

**Rule.** E12: Due diligence may use a later passage "only if it dates the fact".

**What the workbooks wrote.** Opus xhigh #37 codes Light, citing p. 38. Astra xhigh (#53) codes Light on the 21–23 September revision with Due diligence Not stated. The others code Unclear.

**Verdict: Pro is right on the 18 September row.** The only confirmatory statement about CSC is dated after the bid. The 21–23 September rows are closer, because the exclusivity then agreed was for finishing the deal, but still rest on the later passage. K 0.25 for Opus xhigh. This is the concrete case for `03` A3.

### Issue 9. Providence & Worcester: "Not begun" diligence before data-site access

**Filing.** p. 29, after the 1 June selection: the committee authorized management presentations "and allow these parties to conduct **additional** due diligence. These potential buyers were given access to an internet data site." For Party C: "After executing a confidentiality agreement, Party C was provided the memorandum … On July 12, 2016, Party C submitted an IOI … Subsequently, Party C was provided access to the internet data site."

**Rule.** E12: "**Not begun**: the bidder had not yet had diligence access when it bid … Not begun needs affirmative support … an NDA alone does not show that diligence began." The instruction does not say whether a confidential memorandum is diligence access.

**What the workbooks wrote.** Opus xhigh codes Not begun for the May IOIs and for Party C on 12 July; Opus medium codes Not begun for Party C only.

**Pro.** Penalized both: "Absence of data-room access is not absence of diligence."

**Verdict: split.** For the May IOIs, the filing's word "additional" implies the bidders had already done some diligence, so Not begun lacks affirmative support; K 0.25 for Opus xhigh. For Party C, the filing gives a clean sequence (memorandum, IOI, then data site), so Not begun is defensible. **Instruction gap:** say whether a confidential information memorandum counts as diligence access.

### Issue 10. Providence & Worcester: Regulatory Concern on G&W's 12 August bid

**Filing (p. 32).** On 12 August the board noted that "the STB approval process for G&W and Party B would be different since G&W owned connecting railroads", called STB counsel "to discuss … the risks associated with each process … and the likely timeframe", and "determined that the timeframe for, and the likelihood of, obtaining the required regulatory approval was not materially different".

**Rule.** E12: "**Concern**: the filing reports a regulatory risk for this bid … including a risk the filing applies to every bidder." Examples table: "The board weighs a regulatory risk common to every bidder … → Regulatory Concern."

**What the workbooks wrote.** Opus medium (#56) and Opus xhigh code Concern, and also record the assessment separately. The others code Not stated.

**Pro.** Penalized: a comparative assessment made later, "not a finding of zero regulatory risk and not necessarily a condition communicated with the morning bid".

**Verdict: Fable is right.** The board weighed a regulatory risk on the same day, specifically for the G&W bid, and the rule's own example says that is Concern. Pro's point that it is not a bid condition is correct but beside the point: Concern is not a condition, and E12 says Concern alone does not make a bid Heavy. The instruction could still say more plainly that a comparative assessment is enough (`03` A4).

### Issues where both reviewers already agreed but weighed differently

- **Mac-Gray Party B, 18 September financing.** The 9 September IOI "did not include a firm financing commitment" (p. 35); the committee on 19 September said B "would likely be financing the transaction with … equity from affiliated funds and third party debt capital, which would involve more risk". Opus medium coded Contingent and marked it Inferred; Opus xhigh did the same without the marker. Pro called Contingent "too strong". E12 says a reported source together with no commitment is Contingent, and the committee statement concerns the bid it was reviewing that day. Opus medium's coding is supported; the missing Inferred marker on Opus xhigh is the only error, as Fable already recorded.
- **Padding.** Fable deducted small amounts for inferred contact rows and adviser rows, notably in sTec Astra xhigh. Pro gave no credit for extra rows and no penalty either. This moves no ranking.

## 2. What the disagreement was really about

Most of the gap came from Fable reading rules at the level of the label (is this label permitted?) and Pro reading them at the level of the research variable (does the resulting count or state survive into estimation?). On issues 1, 4, 5 and 7, the label-level reading let an error in the data through. Fable's two wins, issues 2 and 10, are the cases where the rule text answers the question directly and Pro reasoned past it.

## 4. Decisions this leaves for Austin (and Alex where marked)

1. **Cohort closure across a deadline** (issue 1). Adopt the rule in issue 1: a cohort whose entry window straddles a deadline closes at the first transition that covers every member. Wording question only; the research choice underneath is `03` B1 (Alex).
2. **Label when eligibility is established** (issue 2). Confirm that E14 means Did not submit, not Dropped by target. No change needed if confirmed.
3. **Named party versus stated total** (issue 4). Change E3 so that a named party stays inside a cohort when entering it separately would exceed a stated total.
4. **Price on commitment-only revisions** (issue 5). E10 already says blank; add a marker distinguishing commitment-only Bid rows from priced ones (`03` A5) so that downstream counts cannot double them.
5. **What counts as diligence access** (issue 9). Decide whether a confidential memorandum or management meeting counts. This is a research definition: **Alex**.
6. **Comparative regulatory assessments** (issue 10). Confirm the E12 example covers "no materially different risk" statements. Wording only.
