P19: In the ledger: ranges used as recorded.

P20: B.	Use the count that presumes the ordinary sequence (bidders were signers; signers were eligible for the first deadline), with the bounds for robustness checks.

P22: C.	Use one of the bounds (please say which).

P23: In the ledger: Datalink 13 or 16.

P24: Recommended: B. A confidentiality agreement is the gate to the data room and to bid letters, so the presumption is nearly always right; the bounds remain for robustness checks.

P27: Bidders for part of the company

P28: QUESTION

P29: Some parties bid only for a division or selected assets while the target sells the whole company, and some bidders switch to a partial offer. Do they count in whole-company live counts and the auction screen?

P30: WHERE IT ARISES

P31: Kraton, pp. 33–35. The board said it would review proposals for the CST business “in connection with” whole-company proposals, and on 6 July 2021 kept Party K’s chemical-segment proposal alongside the whole-company bids.

P32: Synacor, pp. 32–36. Company G offered for the software business alone, and the committee weighed it against a whole-company sale. On 14 December 2020 Company E switched from a $2.00 whole-company offer to a tender offer for 35% of the shares, which the committee kept weighing until 4 January 2021.

P33: sTec, p. 28. Companies E and F signed confidentiality agreements, then said they wanted only “limited, select assets”.

P34: NOW

P35: E1 records a segment offer as an “Other-scope bid” with no whole-company price, but does not say whether such bidders count. The ledgers count Kraton’s segment bidders and keep Synacor’s Company E live as a whole-company bidder until 4 January. Your notes say the research concerns acquisitions of entire companies.

P36: OPTIONS

P37: A.	Count them like any other participant.

P38: In the ledger: Kraton’s segment bidders count; Synacor’s E counts until 4 January.

P39: B.	Record their offers but keep them out of whole-company counts and the auction screen; a bidder that switches to a partial offer leaves at the switch.

P40: In the ledger: Kraton’s first-round count falls by five (C, D, E, G, K); Synacor’s E leaves on 14 December.

P41: C.	Count them, flagged, only when the target treats partial offers as an alternative to a whole-company sale (invites them alongside whole-company bids, or weighs a break-up against those bids).

P42: In the ledger: Kraton: all count. Synacor: G from September 2020, and E (flagged) until 4 January; the separate 2019 portal-only outreach does not count.

P43: Recommended: C. A division bidder competes with whole-company bidders only when the target is willing to sell in pieces; then leaving it out would understate competition. The flag keeps both counts recoverable.

P82: Revised prices with unstated terms

P83: QUESTION

P84: When a bidder revises only its price and the filing is silent on its other terms, does the revision inherit the earlier formality and conditions?

P85: WHERE IT ARISES

P86: Providence and Worcester, pp. 29–30. G&W raised its offer from $21.15 to $22.15 on 26 July 2016 after a letter with markups; the target later negotiated from those markups. Party D, re-engaged after being dropped, sent $24.00 on 1 August with a 30-day diligence period and no mention of a markup.

P87: sTec, pp. 30–32. WDC’s 10 June 2013 range said it was “otherwise prepared to move forward on the transaction terms previously proposed on May 28”, when it had sent a markup. Your coding has 10 June as informal.

P88: Mac-Gray, pp. 35–36. Party B’s 9 September 2013 bid “did not include a firm financing commitment”; its 18 September revision is silent on financing, yet the ledger codes it Heavy by carrying that fact forward.

P89: NOW

P90: E11 needs a markup “with a priced proposal or expressly in support of one”; E12 says silence about financing establishes nothing. Neither settles what a silent revision inherits, and the ledgers are inconsistent.

P91: OPTIONS

P92: A.	Carry both forward, marked as inferred, unless the filing reports a change.

P93: In the ledger: G&W Formal, Heavy; Party D Formal; WDC Formal; Mac-Gray’s Party B Heavy.

P94: B.	Carry formality only when the bidder refers back to its earlier terms or the target is reported negotiating from its markup; code conditions only from what is reported about the revision, otherwise Unclear.

P95: In the ledger: G&W Formal, Unclear; Party D Informal, Heavy (its diligence period is reported); WDC Formal, Unclear; Party B Unclear.

P96: C.	Code each revision only from what is reported about it.

P97: In the ledger: G&W, Party D and WDC Informal; conditions as in B.

P98: Recommended: B. The bid stays formal where the filing ties the new price to the markup, and no bidder looks more or less committed than the filing shows.

P129: QUESTION 7 OF 7

P130: Deadlines and invited improvements

P131: QUESTION

P132: After a bid due date, the target or its banker goes back to bidders and they raise their offers days later. Was that deadline enforced, or were late bids accepted?

P133: WHERE IT ARISES

P134: Mac-Gray, pp. 36–37. Bids were due on 18 September 2013; on 21 September the committee offered CSC/Pamplona exclusivity at $21.25, and CSC/Pamplona made that its “last and best” offer.

P135: PetSmart, p. 24. Indications were due on 30 October 2014. In calls from 30 October to 2 November the banker discussed prices with all bidders, and Bidder 2 raised $78.00 to $81.00–$84.00; the board admitted it on 3 November.

P136: NOW

P137: E9: Enforced if the target acted on the bids in hand; Late bids accepted if it considered a bid that arrived after its due date. Mac-Gray is coded Enforced; PetSmart is coded Unclear, because the day of Bidder 2’s revision is not reported.

P138: OPTIONS

P139: A.	Late bids accepted only when a bidder missed the due date and its first response was still considered; improvements the target invites afterwards are bargaining within the round, recorded as their own rows.

P140: In the ledger: both Enforced; PetSmart no longer depends on the unreported day.

P141: B.	Late bids accepted whenever the target considers any bid made after the due date.

P142: In the ledger: both Late bids accepted; deadlines look soft far more often.

P143: C.	Invited improvements after the target has acted on the bids in hand are negotiation; before it acts, they are late bids.

P144: In the ledger: Mac-Gray Enforced; PetSmart depends on the unreported day.

P145: Recommended: A. “Late bids accepted” should mark a deadline that a bidder could ignore, the evidence on soft deadlines you asked for; bargaining the target reopens is already its own event.
