## 1. Verdict

The instruction matches Alex on **structure**: one row per event with a sequence number, contacts vs NDAs, no memorandum-as-NDA, disjoint residual cohorts (his 20−4=16 Mac-Gray arithmetic is INSTR example A almost verbatim), rounds inferred from target decisions rather than banker vocabulary, process vs round (gap-plus-continuity), deadline set / revised / reached and enforcement, signing vs announcement, IOI = one Bid row, markups ⇒ Formal with a separate none/light/heavy flag, exclusivity not downgrading, final range can be Formal, earn-out = cash + contingent, winner type resolved from elsewhere in the filing, adviser dedupe / rename / first mention, Longview rollover ≠ consortium, grouped review questions. Austin's hypothesis is nevertheless **confirmed, but for one specific family of rules**: the instruction's *closure philosophy*. Wherever the filing is silent or vague, the instruction says "leave it open" (blank Working date on one-sided windows, no exit unless evidenced, exclusivity = pause, a later draft is not a bid, cross-paragraph tightening only if the passage itself constrains the event), whereas Alex says "close it with a best judgment and flag it" (a rough date on every row, every NDA signer eventually gets a Drop/DropTarget, rivals are dropped when exclusivity is granted, a reconfirmed offer becomes a Formal priced row, late July ⇒ Jul 20–22). Pro's review then applies that philosophy *more strictly than the instruction it wrote* (its two "Major" 15−6=9 findings contradict INSTR §10.2's own worked example). Of Pro's 83 findings I judge 19 to be errors Alex would also call errors, 37 things Alex would not care about, 11 things that are what Alex wants, and 16 unclear; of the 15 Majors only 2 are Alex-errors. Pro's caution has real value in three places (double counting, invented facts, invented identities) and works against Alex's use in four (dates, exits, pause-vs-drop, conflict/post-signing coverage). Alex's conventions do carry real risks, and his own documents are inconsistent on several of them, so the right fix is not "do it Alex's way silently" but "fill the value Alex wants **and** carry a basis flag" (§5).

## 5. Risks in Alex's own conventions, with designs that keep them visible

**Q-A. Assigned dates create false orderings.** P&W: "In late July 2016, the Company received six LOIs"; only G&W's is dated (Jul 21, revised Jul 26). Alex dates the other five 07/20, so every rival precedes G&W; Opus's midpoint 07/23 puts G&W's first bid *before* Party B's $24. Either order is an artefact; a regression on "did G&W bid after seeing rivals" would pick it up. Alex's own rule is also unstable: early July→07/01 (start), mid-June→06/15 (mid), "first week of October"→10/07 (end), Oct 30–Nov 2→11/02 (end), "over the next two months"→07/15, "over the next few weeks"→05/23. *Design:* keep Date from/Date to honest; Working date always filled by one deterministic rule (midpoint of a finite window; the bound for a one-sided window; clamp so it never contradicts # order); `Date basis` = Reported / Inferred (cross-paragraph) / Assigned; plus a boolean-like phrase "order vs neighbours not established" where that is true. Alex's Saks comment "[This clearly happened after Final Round Ann -- so the day is approximate]" (HC 7007) shows he already thinks this way.

**Q-B. Residual Drop rows imply decisions nobody observed.** P&W HC 6030 "16 parties · Drop · 06/01/2016": the filing never says 16 parties decided anything on June 1, nor that the 25 is the right base (were Party B and G&W inside the 25? Alex's cohort name says B yes). Dating them at the committee meeting also makes them look simultaneous with the two DropTarget rows. *Design:* one cohort row per transition, label `Did not submit` (round deadline) or `Not selected` (signing), Count = residual, Decided by = Unknown, Exit reason = Not stated, **Exit basis = Inferred (residual arithmetic: 25 − 9)**, Working date = the deadline, and the subtraction written in Terms. Export maps it to Alex's `Drop`. Where the base population is uncertain, still write the row but leave Count blank and flag – never omit.

**Q-C. Dropping rivals at exclusivity.** Mac-Gray: nothing in the filing says A or B were told anything on Sep 24; the exclusivity lasted until Oct 15 and the deal signed Oct 14, so "dropped" is harmless here. In Synacor-type cases (VN VII.1) exclusivity lapses and "many contacts in the same process are renewed", so a hard DropTarget followed by re-entry inflates exits and entries. *Design:* `Dropped by target`, Exit basis = Inferred (exclusivity granted to rival), Related rows → the Exclusivity row; if the bidder later returns, `Re-entered` links back, so a filter can net the pair out.

**Q-D. Upgrading to Formal on an inferred reconfirmation.** P&W Aug 4: what the filing reports is "legal counsel for Party B provided a revised draft of the merger agreement". No price is restated, and "From July 27, 2016 through August 11, 2016, Party B and G&W conducted on-site… due diligence", so "formal unconditional after DD" is not true on Aug 4; the board meeting to approve Party B was set only after "progress made… during the week of August 8". A $24 "bid" on Aug 4 also adds a price observation that was never made. *Design:* `Bid reaffirmed`, Price origin = Carried forward (never Stated), Count 0, Formality Formal (markup signal), Conditions assessed **as of that date** (here Heavy or Insufficient evidence, turning Light at the Aug 12 reaffirmation that all four models already record), Review flag. Alex gets his row and his upgrade path; nobody mistakes it for a second price quote.
