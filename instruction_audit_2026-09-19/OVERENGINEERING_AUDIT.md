# Over-engineering audit — `SEC_Deal_Ledger_Extraction_Instruction.md` (short form, 19 Sep 2026)

Auditor's basis: the instruction read in full (11,713 words by `wc`; 11,478 excluding headings, in 158 paragraph/table blocks, each classified); Alex's collection instructions and March email; his voice notes on eight deals and his own closing summary (the "Claude's reading" section was not treated as his words); his nine hand-collected deals; the three filings; the DeepSeek Providence workbook and the DeepSeek Mac-Gray workbook in the same folder (both produced under the 13,618-word parent, whose ledger rules are the same). Weighting: the parent's six worked examples are all drawn from Providence (they name only G&W and Party B), and the Providence Questions sheet cites "instruction calibration example B (same filing)", so Providence shows what the instruction *costs* but is weak evidence that its rules *work*; Mac-Gray and PetSmart appear in no example and are the cleaner read. Second pass (section 9): the full-batch PetSmart workbook was added, the session logs of both DeepSeek runs were checked, and all workbook figures were re-taken from the final files (the first pass read Mac-Gray a few minutes before the session's last rebuild; one claim that depended on that is withdrawn below). The earlier audit folder was deliberately not read.

## 1. Verdict

Austin's hypothesis is half right, and the half that is right is not the half he stated. The instruction is not mainly hand-holding: only about 6% of it tells a capable model what it would do anyway, and a third of it (about 3,800 words) is research convention that Alex's voice notes show an uninstructed model gets wrong. What is over-engineered is the **output contract**, not the reading guidance: 39 columns, 40 event labels, 15 controlled vocabularies, five date fields, fixed-format strings, tags that repeat fields, a six-block Summary nobody asked for, mandatory Questions coverage, per-field basis labels, and a participant-accounting system (Count semantics, five kinds of "Outcome basis", Count-0 identity rows, per-stage balance). About 45% of the words (defensive edge-case rules 22%, epistemic ceremony 14%, the oversized part of the schema ~9%) serve that contract, and the workbooks show what it costs: for a 4,236-word Providence background the model wrote about 9,250 words of its own (5,391 in two prose columns, 2,009 in Summary, 1,662 in Questions) plus a 5,837-word copy of the source; 54 rows against Alex's 36 (Mac-Gray: 46 against 34; PetSmart: 39 against about 32 once his one-row-per-anonymous-bidder listing is put on a cohort basis; prose volume is the same in all three — 5,391 / 5,086 / 4,354 words in the two text columns, Summaries of 2,009 / 1,819 / 1,635, Questions sheets of 1,662 / 1,648 / 1,582, exactly eight questions each time); a Review flag on 54 of 54 rows (46 of 46 in Mac-Gray); and several rows, and one statement contradicted by the filing, that exist only because a rule demanded them. On the deal that no worked example covered, the conventions did their job — all 13 Mac-Gray priced rows match Alex's hand data on price and formal/informal, as do the three rounds, the deadlines, the NDA arithmetic and the 09/24 drops — which is the evidence that the conventions should stay and the apparatus around them can go. A lean instruction of roughly 5,000–5,500 words (−55%) with a 19-column ledger, ~28 labels and a small rounds table keeps every convention Alex asked for. Going much below ~4,500 words means dropping conventions that demonstrably matter; that floor is where the hypothesis is wrong. The size of the gain should be measured, not argued (section 8).

Does the lean schema preserve the flexibility Alex wants? Yes. In Mac-Gray (the clean deal) all 13 priced rows match Alex on bidder, price or range, date and Formal/Informal, including the final-round range kept Formal (Party A $18–19, his Mac-Gray 6), and the Conditions flag separates the bids he annotated "No firm financing commitment" (Heavy) from CSC/Pamplona's (Light). Two columns do this; the four-part Conditions detail string adds nothing to it. An event order he can trust needs #, a sort date, bounds and the known-order constraint; it does not need two vocabularies describing how the sort date was chosen.

## 5. Rules most likely to hurt output quality

1. **§3.1 basis labels + 40-word quotes + "several premises"; 25 mandates to write into Terms; 31 to explain.** Mean 48 words of Terms and 51 of Why per row; each event is told in Terms (paraphrase), Why (quote), Source text (copy), Summary participants block, and Questions. Alex's own rows have a median of zero comment words. A reviewer who "dislikes bloated ledgers" must read 5,400 words to check 54 rows of a 4,236-word narrative he could read directly.
2. **§11.5 mandatory coverage.** 8 questions averaging ~170 words; every one a "Keep: …" confirmation. The genuinely open Providence calls (is Party A the round-2 non-submitter; is Party D out on 08/02; are E and F one unit; does the 08/04 draft count as a formal $24) are buried in 1,662 words.
3. **§10.1 "A pause does not close a participant" + mandatory Re-entered + §10.2 close-everyone.** Party D is closed twice (Participation paused 08/02, then "Not selected at signing — Inferred: silent" 08/12); Alex codes one Drop on 08/02. This changes a first-order variable: live bidders in the final stage are 4 under the ledger, 3 under Alex. Two Re-entered rows duplicate what the 08/01 bid rows already show (Alex writes "Reengaged" in the bid's comment).
4. **§10.2 Count-0 identity row.** One extra row with 130 words of text, and its "recommended reading" (Party A is the round-2 strategic non-submitter) is the opposite of Alex's considered reading in Providence 11 ("bidder A has never made it out of round one … the strategic bidder that drops out in round two is unnamed, it's not bidder A"). 107 words of rule bought a row that points the wrong way; a Question would have been cheaper and more honest.
5. **§6.3 rejected-anchor rows and `R1 anchor:` tag.** One row that exists only to be rejected; Round opened for round 1 is listed *after* the contacts and NDAs it opens (rows 5–8, same sort date).
6. **§7.2 Count semantics.** Followed in the final workbooks, but it fills cells with bookkeeping prose (why a count is 0, why it is blank) for totals that are derivable.
7. **§8.1 Date basis/method.** Rule violations on 6 rows; effort spent labelling how a sort key was chosen. Oddities follow from rule order: the undated deadline revision is sorted at a computed midpoint (05/08).
8. **§4 Information access changed.** The (a)/(b) test invites the model to construct the differential: row 48's statement about Party B, which the filing contradicts (both bidders had on-site diligence through 08/11).
9. **§9.3 Conditions detail.** Format failures where the source says days, not weeks; "DD open: yes/no" is a second, narrative-based judgment per bid that nobody requested.
10. **§11.4 Summary.** 2,009 words, most of it a fourth retelling; contains calculations ("$3,785,000 ÷ 3% = $126.17m") that no one asked for and that look like data.

Where the extra rows come from. Providence, 54 vs 36: about 8 of the 20 additions are things Alex's voice notes ask for that his hand data lacks (the contact wave, round openings, deadline set/revised/reached, the merger announcement, the NDA cohort split by type); about 10 are rule-induced (rejected anchor, two introductory-meeting contacts, two information-access rows, two Re-entered, the identity row, Party D's second closing, a preferred-bidder Target decision); the other two are pre-NDA first contacts he would probably accept. Mac-Gray, 46 vs 34: 31 rows match Alex; of 15 additions, 4 are his asks (the 50-party contact wave, two deadline-set rows, the announcement) and 11 come from the instruction's own apparatus — three Material process update rows on Moab and the CEO's rollover/support (§4's "one row when it is permitted, denied or formed and one when it is resolved"; Alex has none), three Exclusivity changed rows (request, execution, extension), three sale/target decision rows, one Activist involvement, one Information access changed (Alex keeps that fact as a comment on Party A's NDA row). Alex would likely accept two or three of those (the executed exclusivity, the activist). So roughly 40% of the row inflation is Alex's own requests executed literally and 50–60% is apparatus. Two things Alex has are missing from Mac-Gray despite rules that cover them: the 04/08 approach is labelled Contact rather than Target interest, and BofA's 05/15 termination has no Adviser ended row.

Self-checks do not substitute for the script: the Providence workbook's own check table reports "Every Bid and Bid reaffirmed row has … a fixed-format Conditions detail string — Passed", while `check_ledger.py` fails 6 of 14 on exactly that, plus two forbidden Date basis/method pairs (four in Mac-Gray). That supports keeping mechanical checks out of the prompt, run by the researcher after delivery; it also argues against exact-format free-text fields, which invite cosmetic repair of the string rather than re-reading of the filing — this is no longer hypothetical (section 9).

Caution on attribution: these workbooks come from a mid-tier model under the longer parent (whose worked examples are all Providence, so Providence successes are partly taught, not inferred). The direction of each effect follows from the rule text; the size is what the test must measure.

| Signing, merger announcement and sale-process announcement are three events | Providence 15; Meredith 7; summary I |
| Sort-date rules that use cross-paragraph constraints (due date, committee meeting) | Providence 9: late-July LOIs bounded to July 20–22; PetSmart 1 |

That is about twenty conventions. At ~100 words each plus a 19-column table, a label list and a rounds table, the floor is roughly 4,500 words. A 1,500-word "trust the model" prompt would reintroduce the errors Alex spent eight deals documenting — above all round boundaries, which he calls "the most critical mistakes", and double counting, which he fears will be invisible at estimation. Length as such is also not the enemy: 11,700 words is a small fraction of a frontier context window, and the evidence that rules were "lost in the middle" is thin (two missed labels in Mac-Gray, from a mid-tier model). The measurable harm is output obligations, and those can be cut without touching the conventions.

## 8. What to test rather than argue

Variants, same model, same filings:
- **V0** current short form (11.7k words, 39 columns).
- **V1** lean (≈5k words, 19 columns, rounds table, conditional flags, source numbering done by script).
- **V2** conventions only (section C above, ≈3.5k words) with Alex's own flat schema plus three columns (Round, Conditions, Quote/page) — the strongest form of Austin's hypothesis.
- Optional **V1-frontier**: V1 on a frontier model, to separate "model too weak" from "prompt too heavy".

Design: Providence, Mac-Gray, PetSmart, two or three runs per cell (one run cannot separate prompt effect from sampling noise). Providence is contaminated: many rules, and the parent's worked examples, were fitted to it. Add at least two hand-coded deals the instruction has never seen (Penford and sTec are the most informative: unannounced final round, late reconfirmation, soft deadline, two processes).

Hygiene (section 9 shows why): every run in a fresh session and a clean temporary directory, with no access to earlier workbooks, working files, git history, audit notes, the hand data or the checker; the checker is run by the researcher afterwards. Where a run disagrees with the hand data, adjudicate against the filing before scoring — the hand data has errors too (Alex lists Providence's Party F as financial; the filing says "Party F (a strategic buyer)").

Compare against Alex's hand rows, scored by script:
1. Bid rows: bidder, price low/high, Formality exact match; Formality after the Formal+Heavy→Informal mapping.
2. Round of each bid; number of rounds and processes; round-opening dates.
3. Live bidders at each round boundary and at signing.
4. Order: rank correlation of matched events with Alex's bidderID; count of inversions among bid rows.
5. NDA and contact totals by type; any "at least" where the filing is exact.
6. The catalogue in section 6 as a checklist of 20 known errors: present or absent.
7. Cost: rows (vs Alex's 36/34/53), words per row, Questions count and words, share of rows flagged, share of flags that Alex says he needed, output tokens, checker FAILs.

If V1 loses on rounds or live-bidder counts, restore the specific paragraph whose absence explains the loss, not the whole apparatus.
